# eval-report

Final step of the eval chain: define, analyze, discover, compare, report. Reads every eval file and writes the report that supports the decision. Start the report with a short provenance line: the evaluation date, the scale used, the cost mode, and which steps were rerun after weights or evidence changed. Follow the workspace contract in references/workspace-contract.md.

## Process

1. **Check status.** Read `eval/status.json`. Run `python scripts/eval_calc.py validate eval --before report` and, if it reports a problem, stop and follow its message. If compare is not complete or any step is stale, stop and say which skill to rerun.
2. **Read everything.** Solution, requirements, criteria, options, evidence, scores, features, and the staleness flags in `eval/status.json`.
3. **Open with eligibility.** If any profiled option fails or has an unverified gate, the report opens with "Eligibility: N of M options pass every gate" and the blockers per option, before any score; `report_draft.py` writes this. If no option passes, the recommendation is to shortlist and close the blockers, never to choose an option. The decision table shows the basis (documented, vendor_confirmed or tested) beside each gate pass.
4. **Compare with an earlier assessment (optional).** If the user has another assessment to reconcile with, give `python scripts/eval_calc.py reconcile eval OTHER.csv --map MAP.csv` their criteria, weights and per-option ratings plus a mapping from their criteria to our requirement ids. Report both totals, our ratings under their weights, the per-criterion differences, then the factual disagreements and which side the evidence supports, under "Comparison with an earlier assessment". Do not link to the other report: the evaluation stays independent.
5. **List what to verify.** The report includes the "Verify before deciding" cells from `deciding_cells` in `scores.json`, the POC tests and the open verification items from `eval/open_items.json`; caveats do not repeat them.
6. **Run a freshness sweep.** Before writing, make at most 5 look-ups per must-have standard and per package component for anything new since the evidence was gathered (a new spec version, a release candidate, a release with breaking changes, a security fix that raises a minimum version). Put what you find under "Changes since evidence was gathered" in the risks (the `changes` key of the prose file) and do not change scores without re-running eval-compare. Also list `not_assessed` factors on a "Not assessed:" line; the confidence cannot be high while one is unassessed and the lead is under the meaningful gap.
7. **Keep decision order.** Sections run: eligibility and decision summary (bottom line, scores, blockers, next steps), gates, requirements, weights and scores (category view, sensitivity, per-requirement table), option profiles (strengths, blockers and risks only), POC tests, open items, feature comparison, caveats (at most 10, none repeating a gate or an open item), sources. State each fact in one place. The method history lives in `eval/status.json`, never in the report: do not write "this round" or "re-checked". `report_draft.py check` enforces the order, the caveat limit and the banned wording, and the exporter sorts sections the same way.
8. **Write the report** to `eval/report.md`. Write the judgment text into a JSON file outside `eval/` (the keys are listed in `python scripts/report_draft.py --help`: recommendation, confidence, confidence_why, options, bias, cost, sensitivity, next_steps, risks, excluded), then run `python scripts/report_draft.py draft eval --prose <that file>`. It writes every computed section (tables, scores, costs, sources) from the evaluation files, so no number is typed by hand. The sections, in order, and what each holds:
   - **Decision recommendation:** the recommended option, in one or two sentences, with the confidence level, stated as high, medium, or low and never as a percentage, and the main condition under which the recommendation would change. High means a clear margin that holds under every sensitivity test and the key scores rest on independent evidence, medium means the margin holds but some key scores are low-confidence, and low means a near-tie, a flip under sensitivity, more than half of the total weight resting on low-confidence scores, or unknown requirements that could let another option overtake the leader (`could_overtake_leader` in `eval/scores.json`).
   - **Decision table:** options ranked by weighted score, with gate status, low-confidence flags, and one line on each option's main strength and main gap.
   - **Context:** the solution, who it is for, and the date of the evaluation.
   - **Criteria and weights:** the table the user approved.
   - **Weighted score matrix:** scores per criterion, contributions, and totals.
   - **Bias and assumptions:** from `bias_checks` in `eval/scores.json`: the providers behind the candidates, any requirement based on an assumption, how many findings are vendor-published against independent, and how many options the cost comparison covers. Say plainly what these limit about the recommendation.
   - **Cost:** the assumptions, the total cost of ownership per option over the horizon, the volume scenarios and which option is cheapest in each, and any component priced from unverified or missing evidence.
   - **Criteria and scores:** weighted categories with their requirements, each category's attainment and contribution, and the per-requirement scores with their rationale.
   - **Feature comparison:** the descriptive table from `eval/features.json`, grouped by category, labelled as an inventory that does not feed the scores, with a must-have score summary beside it.
   - **Sensitivity:** whether the ranking holds when influential weights move.
   - **Recommended next steps:** two to four concrete actions that follow from the decision, such as a proof of concept on the riskiest requirement, questions to put to the vendor, or evidence to refresh before committing.
   - **Risks and open questions:** unverified claims that touch the recommendation, gaps, assumptions, and any evidence flagged stale, with its gathered date.
   - **Excluded options:** each with the gate it failed and the evidence.
   - **Sources:** every source used, with date and confidence label, and the date evidence was gathered.
9. **Check the report.** Run `python scripts/report_draft.py check eval` and fix every problem it lists (unfinished prose, a missing section, a missing or wrong provenance line, a decision table whose order or scores disagree with `eval/scores.json`, a recommendation that names another option first or quotes the wrong lead, a near-tie called decisive, a confidence given as a percentage, an excluded option missing from 'Excluded options'). If Python is unavailable, write the report by hand in the same order.
10. **Be honest about the recommendation.** Confidence is high only if no scenario in `scores.json` `scenarios` changes the leader and the lead is at least `meaningful_gap`; `report_draft.py check` enforces it. If weights were changed after scoring, `status.json` has `post_scoring_weight_change` and the draft states it with the reason. If the top two options are within the tie margin, or the result flips under sensitivity, say the choice is not clear-cut and name what would settle it. Never present a narrow win as decisive.
11. **Review with the user.** Present the recommendation and the decision table. Apply requested edits to wording; changes to scores or weights go back through eval-analyze or eval-compare.
12. **Save the record.** Run `python scripts/eval_calc.py complete eval report`, which records the report date in `eval/status.json`. The evaluation stays active in `eval/` until a new evaluation starts, at which point it is archived to `eval-archive/<solution-slug>-<YYYY-MM-DD>/`.
13. **Export.** Offer HTML, a spreadsheet, and PDF. On a yes, run `python scripts/export_report.py eval --formats html,xlsx,pdf` (drop the formats the user did not ask for). It writes `eval/export/report.html`, `report.xlsx`, and `report.pdf` from the computed files. The spreadsheet keeps the weights as editable cells and the totals as live formulas, so the user can retune the ranking. The HTML is collapsible, with a section index and a source filter. The PDF is printed from a fully expanded copy of it by Edge or Chrome; if neither is installed the script says so, and you tell the user to open the HTML and print it to PDF. Report which files were written. Exports are generated from the evaluation files and are not edited by hand.

## Quality rules

- The report adds no facts that are not in the evidence file, apart from context the user supplied in the solution file.
- Keep the recommendation and the evidence consistent. Anything marked UNVERIFIED that the recommendation depends on appears under risks.
- Quote sources briefly. Do not reproduce long passages.
- `report_draft.py` keeps the report consistent with the computed files; `check` is the gate before review.
- The exporter rebuilds the decision table, matrices, cost, sensitivity, excluded and sources sections from the computed files and drops the Markdown tables under those headings, so write the prose (recommendation, context, next steps, risks) and never fix a number in the report by hand: change the evaluation files and rerun the step.
- Open the report's first line with `Evaluation date <date>. Scale 0 to <n>. Cost mode: <mode>.` followed by the reruns and how the evidence was gathered; the HTML shows that line as its opening note.

## Output

Reply in a few sentences: the recommendation, how confident it is, the single biggest risk, and where the report is saved.
